Story

Built in a
family garage.

Racing runs in the family. His grandfather Wayne Sr. opened the family auto shop in 1978 and drag raced with his friends. His mother Sonia held an NHRA license and ran drag strips across Pennsylvania, New Jersey and Delaware. When Lavar was four, his grandfather built him a go-kart and pushed him around the track before he was even old enough for an engine.

From quarter midgets at Blackbird Speedway at age five to a 600cc Micro Sprint at thirteen, Lavar earned every lap. The 2019 NASCAR Driver Development Combine put him in a Legend Car with Rev Racing. Five seasons later he left ARCA with Rookie of the Year honors in 2024 and a runner-up finish in the drivers championship.

He debuted in the NASCAR O'Reilly Auto Parts Series in 2025 with Alpha Prime Racing, qualifying 22nd and finishing 19th at both Dover and World Wide Technology Raceway. Now he's full-time in the No. 45 for the 2026 season, one of only three Black drivers at NASCAR's national level, carrying his family, his hometown, and every kid who never saw themselves in this sport.

"There was no Plan B." Lavar Scott

Urban Affairs Coalition

More than a
race car.

In June 2026, Alpha Prime Racing and the Urban Affairs Coalition launched a workforce development program built around UAC's Team Racing-2-Education. The idea is simple: use a real race team to show young people that motorsport is a career, not just a Sunday afternoon. Lavar's No. 45 carries it all season.

A NASCAR team is a workforce. Behind every car there are engineers, mechanics, data analysts, fabricators, logistics crews, marketers and content teams, and almost none of those jobs are visible from the grandstand. The program puts students inside a live season to see them up close, with mentoring and hands-on time rather than a tour.

Lavar came up the same way, from the Philadelphia area to a national series seat. The program exists so that route is not a one-off.
