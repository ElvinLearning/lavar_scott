Chapter 01 · 1903 · The beach

It started on the sand.

Before there was a speedway, there was a beach: hard-packed sand, wide as a runway, straight for miles. In 1903 the first daredevils lined up on the shore at Ormond-Daytona and timed themselves against a measured mile.

The finish line was wherever the tide said it was.

Chapter 02 · 1935 · Land speed

The fastest place on Earth.

For three decades the world's land-speed records were set here. The last of them: Sir Malcolm Campbell's Bluebird screaming down the beach at 276 miles per hour in 1935 — on tires, on sand, before seatbelts were taken seriously.

When record hunters left for Bonneville, Daytona kept the speed.

Chapter 03 · 1936–47 · The beach-road course

Stock cars. A hotel. A handshake.

In 1936 the city ran its first stock car race on a 3.2-mile loop: half beach, half highway A1A. Drivers slid through the sand turns two wheels from the surf.

Then in December 1947, in the Streamline Hotel on the Daytona Beach oceanfront, Bill France Sr. gathered drivers, owners and promoters and founded NASCAR. The sport was born a few hundred yards from the tide line.

Chapter 04 · 1959 · The Speedway

Then France poured the banking.

Daytona International Speedway opened in 1959: a 2.5-mile tri-oval with turns banked so steep you can't walk up them. The first Daytona 500 finished in a photo so close it took three days to name Lee Petty the winner.

Chapter 05 · 1979–98 · Legends hour

America tunes in.

1979: the first 500 broadcast live flag-to-flag ends with a last-lap crash and a fistfight in turn three, in front of a nation snowed in and glued to CBS. Stock car racing goes national on Daytona's back.

1998: after twenty years of heartbreak, Dale Earnhardt finally wins the 500 — and every crew member on pit road lines up to shake his hand.

Chapter 06 · 1998 · Lights on

Then the sun stopped ending the party.

In 1998 Daytona flipped the switch on the largest lighted sports venue in the world at the time — miles of towers turning the tri-oval into a bowl of daylight at 9 p.m. The summer race moved under the lights and never came back.

Superspeedway racing at night: 190 mph, three-wide, in a stadium glow you can see from the beach where it all started. There is nothing else like it in American sport.

Chapter 07 · 2023–26 · The 45 arrives

Lavar's Daytona.

February 2023: Lavar Scott's first superspeedway start — ARCA, with Rev Racing, on the same 31-degree banking. February 2026: he rolls off for the United Rentals 300, the opening race of his first full-time national season with Alpha Prime Racing.

A kid from Carneys Point, New Jersey, in the No. 45, on the sand where American speed was invented.
